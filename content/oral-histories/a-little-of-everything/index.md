> "We used to grow a little of everything. Wheat, sure, but oats too, and alfalfa for the animals.
> Mom's garden fed us most of the summer. Now you drive out there and it's wheat as far as you can
> see. It happened so slow you almost didn't notice."

**Narrator:** Ruth (pseudonym) · **Region:** North Central Kansas · **Role:** Retired farmer and
long-time resident · **Recorded:** August 2026

In this conversation, Ruth walked us through the farm she grew up on and later worked with her
husband — a place that once carried several crops and a mix of livestock. She described how, over
several decades, the farm came to concentrate on far fewer crops, and connected that shift to changes
she saw all around her.

Among the threads she returned to:

- **Machinery and labor.** As larger equipment arrived and hired help became harder to find, the
  crops that were simplest to plant and harvest at scale gradually crowded out the rest.
- **Markets and prices.** She recalled how the reliable buyers, the local elevator, and the prices on
  offer shaped what it made sense to plant from one year to the next.
- **A thinning community.** Ruth tied changes in the fields to changes in town — a consolidated
  school, fewer young families staying on, and neighbors' farms joining together.

She was careful not to cast these changes as simply good or bad. "People did what made sense at the
time," she said. "You can miss the way it was and still understand why it went."
